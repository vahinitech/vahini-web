Our insight

The invisible stroke.

A camera can photograph a finished page. It cannot see hesitation, writing flow, pressure or confidence, those signals vanish the moment the stroke is finished. That motion is the richest part of handwriting, and it is invisible to every tool built around a lens. We built a pen that feels it, and a software layer that turns the signal into coaching, accessibility, digitisation and screening. The handwriting analyser is live today; the rest we are building in the open, labelled honestly.

How it started

From a real barrier to a research mission.

It began in May 2023 with one question: could technology preserve the natural act of writing while making it more accessible?

1
An accessibility barrierWatching visually-impaired students depend on a scribe in paper exams, held back by something unrelated to their knowledge.
2
A relative in painIn Narasaraopet, a young relative knew the answers but suffered hand pain and slow, uneven writing. Why do some children struggle with speed and formation when others do not?
3
A deep-tech buildReading motion on ordinary paper meant learning hardware, firmware, AI and data science at once. Six months of prototypes, a granted patent, and mentorship at RTIH, Tirupati.

How we work

Three principles.

01

Build for paper first

People already write on ordinary paper. We meet them there, no tablet, no special notebook, instead of asking them to change.

02

Own the signal

The motion data is the moat. We gather it consent-first, with zero image retention in the demo, because the asset is only worth it built the right way.

03

Ship what is ready

Every solution carries a true status, live, in development, or research. We never dress research as product.
