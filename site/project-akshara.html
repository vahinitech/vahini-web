Why it doesn't exist yet

Everyone photographed the page. Nobody recorded the hand.

Decades of handwriting research produced mountains of scanned images and tablet scribbles. Both miss the thing that matters: the living motion of a hand shaping a letter on real paper, the pressure, the pace, the hesitations, the flow. That motion is where learning shows, where health shows, where identity shows. It has never been captured at scale in any language, and for Indic scripts it has barely been captured at all.

Generative AI settled the argument about what such gaps are worth: whoever owns the dataset owns the model. The handwriting-motion corpus is one of the last great datasets still unclaimed. Someone will build it. We decided it should be us, and it should start in India.

A marathon, not a sprint

The brave part is the patience.

There is no shortcut. Every sample in this dataset is written by a real human hand, page after page, school after school, year after year.

Most companies will not commit to something that takes years before it pays. Vahini took a firm stand: the dataset is the company. We build the pen, we run the analyser, we ship products along the way, and all of it feeds one long project that compounds every single day. By the time anyone else starts, this corpus will be years ahead, and years are the one thing money cannot compress.

How collection works

Simple on the surface. Careful underneath.

Schools partner with us

Teachers know handwriting better than anyone, and classrooms are where it happens every day. Partner schools run normal writing practice; students write what they would write anyway, on ordinary paper.

Consent comes first, always

Participation is opt-in, explained in plain language, with guardian consent for every child. A school, a family or a writer can step out at any time, and their data goes with them.

Motion is recorded, not identities

The pen records how writing moves: pressure, pace, flow. Sessions are separated from names before they ever join the dataset. We collect the movement of writing, and we protect the writer.

Every page is quality-checked

A dataset is only as good as its worst sample. Every session passes quality review before it counts, and the corpus stays balanced across scripts, ages and regions by design.

Contributors benefit immediately

Participating students and schools get handwriting coaching reports from day one. The dataset gives back before it asks for anything more.

What the corpus unlocks

One dataset. Four industries.

Handwriting that types itself

Write on paper in your own language and script; the words arrive as digital text. For Indic scripts this simply does not exist today. Akshara is how it gets built.

Learning, seen early

Struggles with writing show up in motion long before they show up in grades. Objective, affordable screening for every classroom, not just the ones with specialists.

Assistive writing

For hands that shake or tire, motion understanding means real help: writing that stays legible, progress that can be measured, therapy that can be guided.

A research foundation

Curated releases for universities and labs, so an entire field can finally study writing the way it actually happens: in motion, on paper, across languages.

For investors

Why this compounds, and why it can't be copied

It cannot be scraped. This data does not exist on the internet. It is created, physically, by hands writing on paper with our instrument. There is no shortcut for a competitor to take, and no archive for them to buy.

It compounds daily. Every consented page makes the models better; better models make the products better; better products bring more writers. The flywheel is already turning: the analyser is live, the pen is patented and in development, and collection is underway.

The moat is triple-layered. Granted hardware IP (Indian Patent No. 584433), a head start measured in years, and relationships with schools that trust us because we earned it consent-first. Each layer protects the others.

And the timing is now. Every vertical is racing to own its proprietary data. Handwriting motion, in the world's most linguistically rich market, is still unclaimed. In five years it will belong to someone.

Responsible by design

Built the slow way, because it's the right way

Children are involved, so the bar is high and we set it ourselves before anyone asked: explicit consent in plain language, guardian sign-off for minors, motion signals separated from identities, the right to withdraw at any time, and community benefit flowing back from day one. A dataset built with schools only works if schools can trust it for years. That trust is part of the asset.
